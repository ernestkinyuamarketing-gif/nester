New Meta Brand Building Ad Formats for 2026

Meta introduced Super Burst, First Impression Ads, expanded Reels Trending Ads and Aggregated View Time at its Brand Building Summit. Here's what each format does.

You've probably heard Meta announce new brand building tools, but what do they actually do for your campaigns? Meta brand building ad formats like Super Burst and First Impression Ads promise to put your brand in front of more people at key moments, but the details matter if you're deciding where to spend your budget. By the end of this post, you'll understand how each format works, what it costs, and whether it fits your goals.

In short

  • Meta announced four new brand building ad formats at its Brand Building Summit on 15 October 2026: Super Burst, First Impression Ads, expanded Reels Trending Ads, and Aggregated View Time (Meta for Business news, checked 23 October 2026).
  • Super Burst concentrates your ad delivery into short, high-impact windows, while First Impression Ads guarantee your brand appears first when someone opens their feed.
  • These formats are designed for brand awareness and reach, not direct conversions, so they suit different goals than performance campaigns focused on sales or leads.
  • The announcement targets brand marketers specifically, signalling Meta's push to offer tools beyond its traditional performance advertising strengths.

What are Meta's new brand building ad formats?

Meta announced four new brand building products at its Brand Building Summit on 15 October 2026: Super Burst, First Impression Ads, expanded Reels Trending Ads, and Aggregated View Time (Meta for Business news, checked 23 October 2026). These formats are designed specifically for brand marketers who want to build awareness and reach, rather than drive immediate conversions like sales or sign-ups.

Super Burst

Super Burst concentrates your ad delivery into short, high-impact windows. Instead of spreading your budget evenly across a day or week, this format pushes your ads out in bursts — showing them to more people in a shorter time. Think of it like a shop opening sale that creates a rush of attention, rather than a steady trickle of visitors throughout the month. Meta has reported this approach, but we could not confirm technical details like minimum spend or exact delivery windows from an official source.

First Impression Ads

First Impression Ads guarantee your brand appears first when someone opens their Facebook or Instagram feed for the day. You're buying that top position, so your ad is the first thing a user sees. This format suits campaigns where being noticed early matters — for example, a product launch or an event announcement. Again, Meta announced this product, but pricing and availability details were not confirmed in the sources we checked.

Expanded Reels Trending Ads and Aggregated View Time

Meta also expanded Reels Trending Ads and introduced Aggregated View Time as a new metric for brand campaigns. These tools aim to help advertisers measure attention and engagement beyond clicks and conversions. How do Super Burst and First Impression Ads work?

Super Burst and First Impression Ads use different mechanics to achieve brand visibility, but both aim to make your brand memorable at specific moments.

Super Burst mechanics

Super Burst concentrates your ad delivery into short, intense bursts rather than spreading it evenly over time. Think of it like turning up the volume on a radio for a few minutes instead of playing it quietly all day. The format pushes your ads to more people in a compressed window, creating a spike of attention.

Meta announced this product at the Brand Building Summit on 15 October 2026 (Meta for Business news, checked 23 October 2026), but we could not confirm technical details like minimum spend, exact burst duration, or targeting options from an official source. The intended use case is clear: campaigns where concentrated attention matters more than sustained reach. For example, a film release might benefit from a burst on opening weekend, or a flash sale might need maximum visibility in a single afternoon.

First Impression Ads mechanics

First Impression Ads guarantee your ad appears first when someone opens their Facebook or Instagram feed for the day. You are buying that guaranteed first position. Once a user has seen your ad, they will not see it again that day in the first position, so the format prioritises fresh reach over frequency.

This format suits product launches, event announcements, or any campaign where being noticed early in the day matters. When should you use these formats instead of performance campaigns?

Super Burst and First Impression Ads serve a different purpose than performance campaigns. They were introduced specifically for brand marketers at Meta's Brand Building Summit on 15 October 2026 (Meta for Business news, checked 23 October 2026), which signals their strategic fit: building awareness and memory rather than driving immediate conversions.

Brand building versus direct response

Performance campaigns optimise for actions like purchases, sign-ups, or app installs. The system finds people likely to convert and delivers ads to them over time. Brand building formats like Super Burst and First Impression Ads optimise for attention and reach instead. You are paying for visibility at a specific moment, not for a predicted action.

Think of it this way: a performance campaign is like fishing where the fish are biting. A brand building campaign is like putting up a billboard where everyone drives past. Both have value, but they achieve different goals.

When brand formats make sense

Use Super Burst or First Impression Ads when your goal is to make people remember your brand, not to drive an immediate sale. Examples include product launches, film releases, event announcements, or seasonal campaigns where concentrated attention matters. If you are a new brand trying to build recognition, or an established brand launching something new, these formats can create a spike of awareness that performance campaigns cannot deliver.

If your goal is measurable return on ad spend (ROAS, meaning revenue for every pound spent on ads), stick with performance campaigns. If your goal is to be noticed and remembered at a specific moment, brand building formats are worth testing.

Common mistakes

  • Treating brand formats like performance campaigns. Super Burst and First Impression Ads are not optimised for conversions, so judging them by cost per acquisition or ROAS will make them look like failures. Instead, measure them with brand-appropriate metrics like reach, frequency, and Aggregated View Time (a metric Meta introduced at the Brand Building Summit on 15 October 2026 to measure how long people actually watch brand ads, Meta for Business news, checked 23 October 2026).
  • Neglecting creative quality. When you pay for guaranteed visibility, poor creative wastes that investment. If your ad appears first in someone's feed but looks dull or irrelevant, you have bought attention but not memory. Brand formats demand stronger creative because the placement does the heavy lifting of getting noticed.
  • Running brand campaigns without a clear awareness goal. If you cannot explain what you want people to remember, a brand format will not help. Define the single message or association you want to build before you spend on concentrated reach.
  • Failing to align measurement with the objective. Performance metrics like click-through rate or conversion rate miss the point of brand building. Use brand lift studies, recall surveys, or attention metrics instead to understand whether the campaign worked.

Frequently asked questions

Are Super Burst ads available in all Meta ad accounts?

Meta has not confirmed whether Super Burst ads are available to all advertisers or require specific account access. The format was announced at the Brand Building Summit on 15 October 2026 (Meta for Business news, checked 23 October 2026), but official documentation does not yet specify eligibility criteria. Check your Ads Manager or contact your Meta representative to see if the format appears in your account.

How is Aggregated View Time different from standard video metrics?

Aggregated View Time measures how long people actually watch brand ads across Meta platforms. Meta introduced this metric at the Brand Building Summit on 15 October 2026 (Meta for Business news, checked 23 October 2026) specifically for brand campaigns. Unlike standard video metrics that count views or completion rates for individual ads, Aggregated View Time gives you a total attention measure across your campaign, which helps you understand whether your brand building investment is holding attention.

First Impression Ads were announced at the Brand Building Summit on 15 October 2026 (Meta for Business news, checked 23 October 2026), but the announcement did not specify compatibility with specific campaign types. Since First Impression Ads guarantee a placement rather than optimising for conversions, they may not align with Advantage+ automation, but this remains unconfirmed.

Do Reels Trending Ads require a minimum budget?

Meta announced expanded Reels Trending Ads at the Brand Building Summit on 15 October 2026 (Meta for Business news, checked 23 October 2026), but has not published minimum budget requirements in official sources we could verify. Brand building formats typically cost more than standard placements because they guarantee visibility, so expect higher entry costs than performance campaigns, but exact thresholds have not been confirmed.

Recap

Meta has introduced four new brand building ad formats designed to help advertisers create lasting awareness rather than just immediate clicks. Super Burst ads deliver concentrated reach in a short window, First Impression Ads guarantee the first placement someone sees each day, Reels Trending Ads now reach more countries, and Aggregated View Time measures total attention across campaigns. These formats work best when paired with strong creative, clear brand goals, and the right measurement approach. They cost more than performance placements because they guarantee visibility, so budget carefully and test whether the brand lift justifies the investment. Brand building takes time, but these tools give you more control over where and how often your message appears.
