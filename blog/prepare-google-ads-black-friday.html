How to Prepare Google Ads for Black Friday (Start Now)

Learn how to prepare Google Ads for Black Friday with a practical checklist covering campaign structure, Performance Max, Shopping feeds, budgets, and testing.

Black Friday is one of the biggest shopping days of the year, and if you want your ads to perform well when it matters most, you need to prepare google ads for black friday weeks before the rush begins. In this guide, we will walk you through exactly how to get your Google Ads campaigns ready so you can capture more sales, avoid wasting budget, and stay ahead of your competitors. By the end, you will know which settings to adjust, what to test, and when to make each change so your campaigns are firing on all cylinders when shoppers start searching.

In short

  • Start preparing your Google Ads campaigns for Black Friday at least four weeks early so you have time to test, adjust, and scale before the shopping rush begins.
  • Structure your campaigns carefully by deciding whether to create separate Black Friday campaigns or adjust existing ones, and choose the right campaign types (Performance Max, Shopping, or Search) based on your products and goals.
  • Test everything in October when traffic is lower—ad copy, landing pages, conversion tracking, and bidding strategies—so you're not fixing problems when shoppers are ready to buy.
  • Plan your budget increases and bidding adjustments in advance, understanding how Smart Bidding responds to traffic spikes and when to raise your daily spend limits.
  • Use Google's campaign structure guidance and optimization tools to organize ad groups by product category or promotion type, making it easier to manage performance during peak demand.

How do I structure Google Ads campaigns for Black Friday?

When Black Friday approaches, one of the first decisions you need to make is how to organize your campaigns. Should you create brand new Black Friday campaigns or adjust the ones you already have? The answer depends on how different your Black Friday offers are from your everyday promotions and how much control you want over your budget.

Separate campaigns versus adjusting existing ones

If your Black Friday discounts are significantly different from your regular offers—say 40% off instead of your usual 10%—creating separate campaigns gives you more control. You can set a specific budget just for Black Friday, write ad copy focused entirely on the sale, and pause these campaigns the moment the event ends. This approach also makes it easier to measure Black Friday performance on its own.

On the other hand, if you are running similar products with slightly deeper discounts, you might adjust your existing campaigns instead. Update your ad copy to mention Black Friday, increase your bids, and raise your daily budget. This saves setup time and keeps your campaign history intact, which helps Smart Bidding (Google's automated bidding that adjusts bids in real time to hit your goals) learn faster.

Organizing ad groups by category or promotion

Within your campaigns, organize ad groups by product category or promotion type. For example, one ad group for "electronics 30% off" and another for "clothing Black Friday deals". This structure lets you write specific ads for each group and makes it simple to see which categories perform best. According to the Google Ads Help Centre (checked 28 October 2026), clear ad group organization improves relevance and can lower your cost per click.

Performance Max versus Shopping or Search

Google offers several campaign types, and choosing the right one matters. Performance Max campaigns use Google's AI to show your ads across Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, and more—all from one campaign. They work well if you want broad reach and trust automation, but you get less control over where ads appear.

Standard Shopping campaigns show your products with images and prices directly in search results. They are ideal if you have a product feed and want to capture shoppers searching for specific items. Search campaigns show text ads when people search for your keywords, giving you the most control over targeting and messaging.

For Black Friday, many advertisers use a combination: Performance Max for broad reach and Shopping or Search for high-priority products where they want hands-on control. If you are unsure which approach fits your business, our team at Nester can help you choose the right campaign structure

What should I test in Google Ads before Black Friday traffic arrives?

Testing your campaigns before Black Friday is like rehearsing a play before opening night. You want to find and fix problems when the stakes are low, not when thousands of shoppers are clicking your ads. October's quieter weeks give you the perfect chance to test ad copy, landing pages, conversion tracking, and bidding strategies so everything runs smoothly when peak demand hits.

Test ad copy and headlines

Start by testing different ad headlines and descriptions to see which ones get more clicks and sales. For example, try "40% Off All Electronics" against "Black Friday Sale: Electronics" to see which message resonates. Google Ads lets you add multiple headlines to one ad, and the system automatically shows the best-performing combinations. According to the Google Ads Help Centre (checked 28 October 2026), testing at least three different ad variations helps you find the strongest message before high-traffic periods.

Run these tests for at least two weeks to gather enough data. If one version clearly wins, pause the weaker ones and use the winner as your foundation for Black Friday.

Check your landing pages load fast and convert

Your landing page is where clicks turn into sales, so it must load quickly and make buying easy. Test your page speed using Google's PageSpeed Insights tool. If your page takes more than three seconds to load, you risk losing impatient shoppers—especially on mobile.

Also test different landing page elements: Does a red "Buy Now" button work better than a green one? Does showing customer reviews increase purchases? Make one change at a time so you know what caused any improvement. For inspiration on how testing improves results, see examples of campaigns we've optimized at Nester.

Confirm conversion tracking works correctly

Conversion tracking tells Google which clicks led to sales, sign-ups, or other valuable actions. Before Black Friday, place a small test order yourself or ask a colleague to do so. Then check your Google Ads account to confirm the conversion appears. If it doesn't, your tracking is broken, and you're flying blind.

The Google Ads Help Centre (checked 28 October 2026) recommends using Google Tag Manager to install your conversion tracking code, as it makes updates easier and reduces errors.

How do I set Google Ads budgets and bids for Black Friday?

Budget and bid management during Black Friday is about giving your campaigns enough fuel to compete without burning through your money too quickly. Think of it like filling a car's tank: you need enough petrol to reach your destination, but you also need to watch the gauge so you don't run out halfway through the journey. The key is planning how much to spend, when to increase it, and letting Google's automated bidding systems adapt to the traffic surge.

Start with a daily budget you can sustain

Before Black Friday arrives, calculate a daily budget you can afford for the entire peak period (typically the week leading up to Black Friday and through Cyber Monday). If you normally spend £50 per day, consider whether you can increase to £100 or £150 during this window. According to the Google Ads & Commerce blog (checked 28 October 2026), new priority and allocation features let you guide how your budget is distributed across campaigns, so you can favour high-performing products or audiences.

A simple approach: take your total Black Friday budget, divide it by the number of days you'll run at increased spend, and set that as your new daily limit. For example, if you have £1,400 for a seven-day period, set your daily budget to £200.

Increase budgets gradually, not all at once

Raising your budget from £50 to £200 overnight can confuse Google's Smart Bidding algorithms, which rely on recent performance data to set bids. Instead, increase your budget in steps over a week or two. Start by lifting it 20 to 30 per cent, let the system adjust for a few days, then increase again. This gradual ramp helps Smart Bidding learn how to spend the extra money efficiently.

The Google Ads Help Centre (checked 28 October 2026) notes that Smart Bidding strategies like Target ROAS (return on ad spend, meaning the revenue you want for every pound spent) and Maximise Conversions automatically adjust bids in real time as traffic patterns change, so they respond well to budget increases if given time to adapt.

Not every campaign deserves the same budget boost. Look at your account over the past month and identify which campaigns deliver the lowest cost per acquisition or highest ROAS. Common mistakes

Even experienced advertisers make avoidable errors when preparing for Black Friday. Here are the most common pitfalls and how to fix them:

  • Launching new campaigns too late. If you start a brand-new campaign on Black Friday itself, Google's Smart Bidding has no performance history to work with, so it spends the first few days learning instead of converting. Fix: launch new campaigns at least two weeks early so the algorithm can gather data before the rush.
  • Not testing conversion tracking beforehand. You might assume your tracking works, only to discover on Black Friday that purchases aren't being recorded. Fix: place a test order yourself a week before the sale, then check your Google Ads account to confirm the conversion appears.
  • Setting budgets too low to capture demand. If your daily budget runs out by midday, you miss the evening shoppers. Fix: calculate a daily budget that can last the full 24 hours, even on your busiest day, and increase it gradually in the weeks before Black Friday.
  • Neglecting mobile landing page speed. Most Black Friday traffic comes from phones, and a slow-loading page kills conversions. Fix: use Google's PageSpeed Insights tool now to test your mobile site speed, and work with your developer to fix any issues before the sale starts.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start preparing Google Ads for Black Friday?

Start at least four weeks before Black Friday itself. This gives Smart Bidding algorithms time to learn from any new campaigns or budget changes you make, and it lets you test your conversion tracking and landing pages before the rush. According to the Google Ads Help Centre (https://support.google.com/google-ads/, checked 28 October 2026), Smart Bidding strategies adjust bids in real time based on recent performance data, so they need a learning period to work efficiently when traffic patterns change.

Should I create separate Black Friday campaigns or adjust existing ones?

Both approaches can work, but separate campaigns give you more control. A dedicated Black Friday campaign lets you write specific ad copy highlighting your sale, set a distinct budget just for the event, and pause it cleanly when the sale ends without affecting your evergreen campaigns. If you adjust existing campaigns instead, increase budgets gradually and add new ad variations that mention your Black Friday offer, so you keep your performance history while still promoting the sale.

How much should I increase my Google Ads budget for Black Friday?

A typical increase is two to three times your normal daily budget, though the exact amount depends on your goals and how much extra demand you expect. For example, if you usually spend £50 per day, consider raising it to £100 or £150 during Black Friday week. According to the Google Ads & Commerce blog (https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/, checked 28 October 2026), new priority and allocation features let you guide how your budget is distributed across campaigns, so you can direct more spend to high-performing products or audiences during peak periods.

Do I need to change my bidding strategy for Black Friday?

You usually do not need to switch strategies, but you may want to adjust your targets. If you use Target ROAS, consider lowering your target slightly to allow more volume (for example, from 400% to 350%), since competition increases and costs rise during Black Friday. If you use Maximise Conversions with a target cost per acquisition, you might raise your target CPA to stay competitive in the auction. The Google Ads Help Centre (https://support.google.com/google-ads/, checked 28 October 2026) notes that Smart Bidding automatically adjusts bids as traffic patterns change, so small target tweaks are often enough without changing the entire strategy.

Recap

Preparing Google Ads for Black Friday takes planning, but the steps are straightforward. Start at least four weeks early so Smart Bidding can learn from your changes. Set up conversion tracking properly, test it now, and make sure every sale is counted. Increase your budget gradually rather than all at once, write new ad copy that highlights your Black Friday offer, and add negative keywords to block irrelevant clicks. Use audience lists to reach past visitors, schedule ads for your busiest hours, and check your mobile landing page speed so shoppers can buy quickly. Avoid common mistakes like pausing campaigns too early or letting your budget run out by lunchtime. Follow this checklist and you will be ready when the Black Friday rush begins.
