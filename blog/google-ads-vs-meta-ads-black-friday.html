Google Ads vs Meta Ads: Black Friday Budget Guide

Compare Google Ads and Meta Ads for Black Friday. Learn how search captures intent while social generates demand, and build a framework to allocate your budget.

You're planning your Black Friday budget, and you need to decide where your ad spend will work hardest. Choosing between google ads vs meta ads can feel overwhelming when every pound counts during the busiest shopping weekend of the year. By the end of this guide, you'll know which platform suits your goals, how to split your budget, and what to watch for when the sales rush begins.

In short

  • Google Search and Shopping campaigns respond to existing queries from people already looking to buy, while Meta's social placements interrupt browsing to generate interest.
  • Google excels at converting high-intent searches and can prospect through broad match and Discovery, while Meta builds awareness and retargets site visitors with dynamic product ads.
  • Your budget split depends on product type, average order value, existing brand awareness, and attribution window — a fashion retailer with strong search volume needs a different approach than a new direct-to-consumer brand.

How does Google Ads capture purchase intent differently than Meta?

When someone types "black running trainers size 10" into Google, they're already shopping. When someone scrolls through Instagram and sees an ad for running trainers, they might not have been thinking about shoes at all. That difference shapes everything about how these two platforms work.

Search responds to existing demand

Google Search and Shopping campaigns show your ads to people who have typed a query into the search bar. According to the Google Ads Help Centre (checked 28 October 2026), Search campaigns match your keywords to what people are actively looking for, while Shopping campaigns display product images and prices when someone searches for items you sell. If you sell coffee machines and someone searches "best bean-to-cup coffee maker under £200", your Shopping ad can appear with a photo, price, and link to buy. The person has already decided they want to spend money — you're just competing to be their choice.

Meta works the opposite way. Someone browsing Facebook or Instagram isn't searching for your product. Your ad interrupts their feed, hoping to spark interest they didn't know they had. This can work brilliantly for impulse buys, gift ideas, or products people discover rather than hunt for, but it requires creative that stops the scroll.

Google can prospect, but differently

Google isn't only about capturing demand that already exists. Broad match keywords and Discovery campaigns let you reach people earlier in their journey, but they still rely on some signal of intent — a related search term or browsing behaviour that suggests interest. If you're weighing where to invest your Black Friday budget, understanding this intent difference helps you decide which platform matches your product and audience. We explain how to split your spend across both in our paid media services.

What role does each platform play in prospecting vs retargeting?

Both Google and Meta can find new customers and bring back old ones, but they do it in very different ways. Understanding these roles helps you decide where to put your money when Black Friday arrives.

Google prospects through signals, converts through intent

Google Search campaigns work best when someone already knows what they want. If a shopper types "wireless headphones Black Friday deals", they're ready to buy — you're competing on price, reviews, and how quickly you can deliver. According to the Google Ads Help Centre (checked 28 October 2026), broad match keywords let you reach related searches you might not have thought of, while Discovery campaigns show your products to people browsing YouTube, Gmail, and the Discover feed based on their interests and past behaviour. These tools help you prospect, but they still rely on Google knowing the person has shown some interest in your category.

Shopping campaigns can also prospect. When someone searches "gift ideas for runners", your running watch might appear even if they didn't search for watches specifically. The key is that Google starts with a search query — some expression of need or curiosity.

Meta builds awareness and retargets with precision

Meta excels at two things: making people aware of products they didn't know existed, and reminding people about products they've already seen. If you're launching a new brand or selling something people discover rather than search for — like a quirky kitchen gadget or a subscription box — Meta's feed placements let you interrupt someone's scroll with eye-catching creative. You're not waiting for them to search; you're creating the demand.

Retargeting is where Meta shines brightest. Dynamic product ads automatically show people the exact items they viewed on your site, plus similar products they might like. If someone browsed three pairs of trainers on Monday but didn't buy, they'll see those trainers (and a discount code) in their Instagram feed on Tuesday. This works especially well during Black Friday when people are comparison shopping across multiple sites. Real examples of how we've used this approach are in our case studies.

For most e-commerce brands, the best Black Friday strategy uses both: Google to capture people actively searching, Meta to build awareness and retarget browsers who haven't converted yet.

How should you allocate budget between Google and Meta for Black Friday?

There's no universal answer to the Google versus Meta budget question. The right split depends on your product, your brand's existing visibility, and how customers typically find what you sell. Instead of declaring one platform the winner, use this framework to decide what makes sense for your business.

Match your budget to your product and brand awareness

Start by asking: do people search for what you sell, or do they discover it? If you sell products with high search volume — electronics, branded fashion, home appliances — Google deserves a larger share because shoppers are already looking. A fashion retailer with strong search demand might allocate 60–70% to Google Shopping and Search, using Meta to retarget browsers and showcase new arrivals.

If you're a new direct-to-consumer brand selling something people don't yet know they want, flip that split. Imagine a startup selling eco-friendly cleaning tablets. Few people search "cleaning tablets" compared to "washing up liquid", so Meta's feed placements become your primary discovery channel. You might spend 60–70% on Meta to build awareness, reserving 30–40% for Google to catch the smaller group actively searching.

Average order value (AOV) also matters. Higher-ticket items (over £100) often need multiple touchpoints before purchase. Someone buying a £500 coffee machine will research, compare, and revisit. That journey suits a balanced budget: Google to capture their research searches, Meta to stay visible between visits.

Consider your attribution window and conversion path

Attribution windows — how long after seeing an ad someone can convert and still count toward that ad — affect what you measure. If your customers typically convert within a day or two, both platforms work well. If your product has a longer consideration period (a week or more), Meta's retargeting becomes more valuable because you can keep your brand in front of browsers across multiple sessions. Google will capture the final search when they're ready to buy, but Meta helped keep them warm.

The best Black Friday strategies treat Google and Meta as partners, not rivals. Use Meta to build your audience in October, retarget browsers throughout November, and rely on Google to convert high-intent searchers when the sale begins. Adjust the ratio based on what your own data shows, not industry averages.

Common mistakes

Even experienced advertisers make predictable errors when running Black Friday campaigns across both platforms. Here are the key pitfalls to avoid:

  • Treating Google and Meta as either-or choices. Many brands pick one platform and ignore the other, missing the fact that most customer journeys involve both discovery and search. Run both where budget allows, using each platform's strengths rather than forcing a single-channel approach.
  • Neglecting cross-channel attribution. If you measure each platform in isolation, you'll undervalue whichever one assists rather than closes the sale. Someone might discover your product on Instagram, research it on Google, then return via a Meta retargeting ad to buy. All three touchpoints mattered. Use Google Analytics or a dedicated attribution tool to see the full path, not just the last click.
  • Copying creative strategies between platforms without adapting. A text ad that works on Google Search — focused on price, specs, and urgency — will fail on Instagram if you paste the same approach. Meta users scroll for entertainment and inspiration, not to solve an immediate problem. Adapt your messaging and visuals to match how people use each platform, even when promoting the same product.
  • Ignoring mobile experience on Meta. Most Meta users browse on mobile, yet some advertisers send them to desktop-optimised landing pages that load slowly or require pinch-and-zoom. Test your entire funnel on a phone before Black Friday starts.

Frequently asked questions

Should I spend more on Google or Meta for Black Friday?

It depends on whether people already know what you sell. If you're selling products people actively search for (like "wireless headphones" or "winter coats"), put more budget into Google to capture that high-intent traffic. If you're launching something new or selling items people don't typically search for by name, Meta works better for discovery — you can show your product to people based on their interests before they even know to look for it. Most brands see the best results splitting their budget between both platforms, using Meta to build awareness in the weeks before Black Friday and Google to convert searchers when the sale begins.

Can I run the same creative on Google and Meta ads?

No, and you shouldn't try. Google Search ads are text-based and appear when someone is actively looking for a solution, so they need clear product details, price, and a direct call to action. Meta ads appear in feeds while people scroll for entertainment, so they need eye-catching visuals and messaging that stops the scroll — more about lifestyle and benefits than specs. Your offer can stay the same (e.g. "30% off Black Friday"), but the way you present it must match how people use each platform. A text ad optimised for Google will look out of place on Instagram, and a scroll-stopping Instagram video won't fit in a search results page.

How do I track sales across Google and Meta together?

Use Google Analytics or a dedicated attribution tool to see the full customer journey, not just the last click before purchase. Someone might discover your product through a Meta ad, search for it on Google a few days later, then return via a Meta retargeting ad to buy — all three touchpoints contributed to that sale. If you only look at each platform's own reporting, you'll think one platform "won" when really both played a role. Set up conversion tracking that feeds into a central system, and look at assisted conversions (the ads people saw before the final purchase) alongside last-click data to understand what's actually working.

Which platform works better for retargeting Black Friday visitors?

Meta typically performs better for retargeting because its placements appear while people browse casually, keeping your brand visible between visits. If someone views a product on your site but doesn't buy, a Meta ad showing that exact product in their Instagram feed the next day feels natural and timely. Google's retargeting (through Display or YouTube) also works, but Meta's detailed audience options and visual formats make it easier to create personalised reminders. For Black Friday, use Meta to retarget browsers in the days leading up to the sale, and reserve Google for capturing people who search for your product or category by name when they're finally ready to buy.

Recap

Choosing between Google Ads and Meta Ads for Black Friday isn't about picking one winner — it's about using each platform where it works best. Google captures people actively searching for what you sell, while Meta introduces your products to interested shoppers before they even start looking. The right split depends on your products, audience, and goals, but most successful Black Friday campaigns use both platforms together: Meta builds awareness early, Google converts searchers when they're ready to buy, and both platforms retarget visitors who need a reminder. Test your approach before the sale, track results across platforms, and adjust your budget based on what actually drives sales, not just clicks.
